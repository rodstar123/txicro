### STRATEGIC POSITION

**Section Title:** At the Center of the Americas

McAllen's geographic position is its most powerful asset. Situated on the US-Mexico border at the southern tip of Texas, the city sits at the convergence of multiple trade corridors that connect Latin America to the heart of the United States.

To the south, the Reynosa-McAllen gateway provides direct access to Monterrey — Latin America's most important industrial city and home to major manufacturing operations for automotive, aerospace, appliance, and electronics companies. The drive takes two and a half hours, and the economic relationship between the two cities has been deepening for decades.

To the north, McAllen connects to San Antonio, Austin, Houston, and Dallas via major highways — placing it within a day's drive of the largest concentration of economic activity in the southern United States. Goods that cross the border in McAllen can reach Port Houston, the largest import-export port in the country, within five hours.

To the east and west, the Rio Grande Valley stretches along the Texas-Mexico border with a network of international bridges, free trade zones, and logistics infrastructure that handles billions of dollars in cross-border commerce annually.

This is not a border town on the periphery. This is a gateway at the center of the hemisphere's most important trade relationship.

### WHY TEXAS

**Section Title:** Why Texas

Before understanding McAllen, it helps to understand why Texas itself attracts international investment at the scale it does.

**The largest exporting state in the United States.** Texas has led the nation in exports for over two decades. The state's export economy is diversified across petroleum, technology, manufacturing, agriculture, and chemical products.

**Business-friendly regulatory environment.** Texas consistently ranks among the top states for business formation and growth. Streamlined permitting, moderate regulation, and a legal framework that favors commercial activity make the state attractive to both domestic and international operators.

**No state income tax.** Texas is one of only nine states with no personal income tax and imposes no corporate income tax. The franchise tax (margin tax) is among the lowest business taxes in the country.

**Infrastructure at scale.** Texas has more freight railroad miles than any other state, two of the ten busiest ports in the nation, and a highway system that connects every major US market. The energy grid, while imperfect, supports one of the largest industrial bases in the Western Hemisphere.

**Workforce depth.** Texas adds more jobs annually than most countries. The state's population growth — driven by both domestic migration and international immigration — provides a deep and diverse labor pool across skill levels.

### WHY McALLEN SPECIFICALLY

**Section Title:** Why McAllen

Many Texas cities compete for international investment. Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, Austin, and El Paso all have legitimate claims to international business. So what makes McAllen different?

**The Monterrey connection.** No other Texas city has the same depth of economic integration with Monterrey. The McAllen-Reynosa-Monterrey corridor is not a theoretical trade route — it is a living, daily commercial artery with thousands of trucks, workers, and transactions crossing every day. Companies that need proximity to Mexican manufacturing operations find McAllen uniquely positioned.

**Cost advantage.** Office space, warehouse space, housing, and labor costs in McAllen are significantly lower than Houston, Dallas, or Austin — often by thirty to fifty percent. For companies expanding from Latin America, this cost structure allows faster scaling and lower breakeven points.

**Bilingual workforce.** McAllen's workforce is overwhelmingly bilingual in English and Spanish. This is not a niche advantage — it is a fundamental operational capability for any organization doing cross-border business. Bilingual customer service, bilingual documentation, bilingual management — it is the default here, not the exception.

**Foreign Trade Zone #12.** McAllen's FTZ allows companies to defer, reduce, or eliminate customs duties on goods entering the zone. For manufacturers and distributors managing cross-border supply chains, this can translate to significant cost savings.

**Quality of life.** McAllen has been consistently ranked among the safest and most affordable cities in the United States. The warm climate, growing restaurant and cultural scene, and proximity to both US and Mexican metropolitan areas make it attractive for executives and their families — not just for business operations.

**Growing infrastructure.** McAllen-Miller International Airport continues to expand its route network. The McAllen Convention Center hosts major events and can support trade shows and delegation visits. The city's road infrastructure connects efficiently to the international bridges and to the highway system heading north.

### INDUSTRY SECTORS

**Section Title:** Key Industries in the McAllen Region

**Manufacturing & Maquiladoras** — The twin-plant model that pairs US-side operations with Mexican manufacturing facilities has defined the border economy for decades. McAllen's proximity to Reynosa and Monterrey makes it the natural US-side hub for companies operating maquiladora facilities.

**Logistics & Warehousing** — With four international bridges and direct highway access to Houston and San Antonio, the McAllen area is a growing logistics hub. Warehouse and distribution facilities are expanding to serve cross-border supply chains.

**Commerce & Retail** — McAllen is one of the largest retail markets on the US-Mexico border, attracting shoppers and business buyers from both sides. The city's commercial infrastructure supports both domestic and cross-border retail operations.

**Healthcare & Medical Tourism** — The Rio Grande Valley has a growing healthcare sector serving both local and cross-border patients. Medical facilities, specialist clinics, and dental tourism draw visitors from across northern Mexico.

**Agriculture & Food Processing** — The RGV's agricultural heritage remains a significant economic driver. Citrus, vegetables, and specialty crops are produced locally, and the Pharr International Bridge is the number one produce crossing point in the United States.

**Professional Services** — Accounting, legal, consulting, and financial services firms in McAllen serve a client base that spans both sides of the border. Bilingual professional services are in high demand.
